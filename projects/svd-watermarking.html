SVD-Based Image Watermarking and Robustness Analysis

Exploring Singular Value Decomposition for image watermarking and studying the trade-off between image quality and watermark robustness.

Introduction

Digital watermarking is a technique for embedding information into an image while keeping the visual appearance of the original image largely unchanged.

In this project, I implemented an image watermarking pipeline based on Singular Value Decomposition (SVD). A QR-code watermark is embedded into RGB cover images and later recovered from the watermarked image.

The main goal was to study the trade-off between watermark strength, image quality, and robustness against image degradation.

Why SVD?

Singular Value Decomposition represents a matrix as the product of three matrices:

A = UΣVT

The singular values contain information about the magnitude of different components of the image. This makes them useful for embedding additional information without directly modifying individual image pixels.

The watermarking pipeline

The overall process consists of decomposing the cover image and watermark, modifying the singular values, reconstructing the watermarked image, and finally attempting to recover the embedded watermark.

Overview of the watermarking and recovery pipeline.

Embedding the watermark

Let the cover image be represented by C. Its singular value decomposition is:

C = UCΣCVCT

The QR-code watermark Q is similarly decomposed:

Q = UQΣQVQT

The watermark is embedded by modifying the singular values of the cover image:

Σ̃ = ΣC + αΣQ

Here, α controls the watermark strength. Increasing α makes the watermark stronger, but can also increase distortion in the cover image.

The watermarked image is then reconstructed using the original singular vectors:

Cα = UCΣ̃VCT

The same process is applied independently to the three RGB channels.

Implementing SVD from eigendecomposition

One part of the project involved implementing SVD without directly relying on a library SVD routine.

For a matrix M, the Gram matrix can be constructed as:

G = MMT

The eigenvalues of this matrix are related to the singular values of M:

σi = √λi

The resulting eigenvectors and singular values can then be used to construct the SVD components and reconstruct the original matrix.

Recovering the watermark

After obtaining a watermarked image, the embedded watermark component can be estimated from the difference between the original and modified singular values.

Σ′Q = (Σ′ − ΣC) / α

The estimated watermark singular values are combined with the watermark singular vectors to reconstruct the recovered QR image.

Recovery is evaluated using both numerical similarity and whether the recovered QR code can actually be decoded.

Baseline results

Before testing robustness, the basic embedding and recovery pipeline was evaluated on the cover images.

Choosing the watermark strength

The watermark strength α was varied to study how it affects image quality and watermark recovery.

When α is very small, the watermark introduces little distortion, but the embedded information can become more difficult to recover after degradation.

Increasing α makes the watermark stronger. However, increasing the strength indefinitely does not guarantee better recovery because additional distortion and clipping can affect the result.

Robustness to Gaussian noise

Gaussian noise was added to the watermarked images before attempting watermark recovery.

The experiment was used to study how increasing noise affects the quality of the recovered watermark and the ability to decode the QR information.

Robustness to JPEG compression

JPEG compression was used as a second degradation experiment. Different compression quality factors were applied before recovering the watermark.

Evaluation

The experiments use multiple measures to evaluate the watermarking system.

PSNR measures the numerical similarity between images and provides a way to quantify image quality and watermark recovery.

QR decoding provides a more practical test: whether the recovered watermark still contains usable information.

What the experiments showed

The experiments demonstrate a trade-off between watermark strength and image quality. A weak watermark produces less distortion but can be more sensitive to degradation.

A stronger watermark can improve recovery under some degradation levels, but increasing the strength too much introduces additional distortion and does not necessarily provide better recovery.

The results therefore show that the watermark strength should be selected based on the expected degradation rather than simply maximized.

Conclusion

This project provided a practical look at how SVD can be used for image watermarking and how the choice of embedding strength affects both visual quality and robustness.

The project also connected the mathematical properties of SVD with an actual image-processing application, including implementation, reconstruction, degradation, and watermark recovery.
