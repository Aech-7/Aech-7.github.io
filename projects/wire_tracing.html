Wire Tracing with DLS-IK

Teaching a robot arm a 3D path by demonstration, then replaying it autonomously with inverse kinematics.

Harshada Kale·Robotics Coursework · Task 3

Overview

The wire-tracing task focuses on teaching a robot arm to follow a three-dimensional wire path without making contact. Using the Addverb HEAL manipulator, the path is demonstrated manually and then replayed autonomously.

Teaching and trajectory replay

Gravity compensation allows the end effector to be guided by hand along the wire while recording its position and orientation. The recorded waypoints are processed into a trajectory suitable for execution, and a Damped Least Squares Inverse Kinematics (DLS-IK) solver converts the desired end-effector motion into joint commands. Joint-velocity control is used to replay the path.

Key challenge

The robot must reproduce the demonstrated path smoothly and accurately while maintaining clearance from the wire. Trajectory quality, IK stability, and velocity control all affect whether the motion can be executed without contact.

Core idea: record a motion through physical demonstration, process the waypoints, and use DLS-IK to execute the trajectory autonomously on the robot.
